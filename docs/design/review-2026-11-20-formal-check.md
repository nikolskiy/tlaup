# Review of the formal check and of the top-up working-in, 2026-11-20

Two review gates of the [roadmap](../roadmap.md), done by one fresh agent that did not write any of the work under review:

- **The formal check** (decision D46): the three TLA+ specs, their configs and `check.sh` under [`specs/`](../../specs/README.md), the stateful tests under `tests/stateful/`, against [03 part 2](03-run-stages-and-specs.md#part-2-tla-specs-of-the-design) and the design sections the specs model ([11 R1, R2, R6](11-runner.md#r6-run-folders), [20 G3](20-groundwork.md#g3-java-launcher), [12 V1, V3, V4, V11, V12](12-tools-and-downloads.md#v1-version-store)). Commits `c453359` and `ce5352d`.
- **The top-up working-in** (decisions D51 to D59): the design documents and the roadmap as they stand after commit `7fc0e55`, against research 19 to 35, the [top-up review](top-up-review-2026-11-20.md), the [notes](top-up-notes-2026-11-20.md) and the [summary](top-up-incorporation-2026-11-20.md).

The documents were reviewed as they are now; the diffs were used only to see what changed.

**The formal check: ready for sign-off after small fixes.** The specs model the design closely, at the level of detail that matters for the races they are about, and the variants that are expected to fail show what the README says they show. The accepted risks (K1-8, K2-2, K2-3) are stated honestly in R6, V3 and V1. The stateful tests mirror their specs step for step, and the places where they cannot are named with sound reasons. But the check found one real flaw that the specs, the tests and the design share (FC1): a kept run folder can be deleted without `--all`, which D53 forbids. It was out of reach of every config because no config lets more than two runs happen. Its fix is a small rule in R6, one line in the spec and one in the test, and needs no new decision. One store property is vacuous (FC3). The rest are gaps in what is tested, overstated or stale sentences, and documents that disagree about what state the work is in. None of them changes a decision.

**The top-up working-in: ready after small fixes and one decision.** D51 to D59 are written into the sections they affect, and their claims follow from the research, with small overstatements (TU8, TU9). One promise of D52 cannot be kept as designed (TU1): the JVM stays in tlaup's process group, so a Ctrl-C typed at the terminal reaches TLC directly and the checkpoint deferral cannot hold it; the tests and the spec do not see it, because both send or model the signal to tlaup alone. Choosing between a process group of the JVM's own and a narrower promise is the maintainer's. D58 is half built for parsed runs (TU2), and the lifeline jar has no place in CI, the wheel or the roadmap (TU3).

Counts: formal check, one high, five medium, six low (FC1 to FC12); top-up, one high, four medium, five low (TU1 to TU10). The most important are FC1 and TU1.
